# Project LockStep

**Author:** Kabo Ernest Ntalamo
**Project:** Cybersecurity Awareness Campaign  
**Course:** Introduction to Computing in Cybersecurity  
**Assessment:** End Assessment Project  

Project LockStep is a student cybersecurity awareness campaign. Its purpose is to show how everyday users can protect their online accounts from password attacks, phishing, and identity theft by using strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication (MFA).

The campaign encourages users to take simple but effective steps to secure their accounts, recognise suspicious activity, and report possible cybersecurity incidents quickly.

## Campaign Purpose

Project LockStep was created to raise awareness of common cybersecurity threats that affect students and everyday internet users. The campaign focuses on helping users understand that a password alone is not enough to protect an online account.

The main message of Project LockStep is:

**Secure accounts. Safer devices. Greater peace of mind.**

## Cybersecurity Threats Covered

Project LockStep focuses on three common cybersecurity threats:

### 1. Password Attacks

Attackers may guess weak passwords or try passwords that have been exposed in previous data breaches.

**Defence:** Use a long, unique password or passphrase for every account. A password manager can help create and store passwords securely.

### 2. Phishing

Phishing uses fake emails, messages, websites, or calls to trick users into revealing passwords, PINs, or one-time verification codes.

**Defence:** Check the sender and website address before signing in. Do not click suspicious links or share one-time codes.

### 3. Identity Theft

Criminals may use stolen personal information or a compromised account to impersonate someone or access other services.

**Defence:** Protect personal information, secure your email account, and report suspicious activity quickly.

## Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds another verification step after entering a password. This may include an authenticator-app code, passkey, security key, or fingerprint.

MFA is important because it reduces the risk of an attacker accessing an account even if they know the password. Two-factor authentication (2FA) is one type of MFA.

Users should enable MFA on important accounts such as:

- Email
- Student portal
- Financial accounts
- Cloud storage
- Social media accounts

## Security Practices Promoted by Project LockStep

1. **Turn on MFA** on important accounts and use the strongest option available, such as a security key, passkey, or authenticator app.
2. **Use unique passwords** or long passphrases for every account.
3. **Install updates promptly** and enable automatic updates where possible.
4. **Stop and check** before clicking links, opening attachments, approving login requests, or sharing one-time codes.
5. **Report suspicious activity** to the official university IT helpdesk or relevant service provider.

## What To Do If an Account Is Compromised

If you think your account has been compromised, act quickly:

1. Change your password using a trusted device.
2. Sign out of other active sessions.
3. Enable MFA.
4. Contact your university’s official IT helpdesk.
5. If money is involved, contact your bank or mobile money provider through its official app or verified website.
